# ADR 0022: depth-integrated shallow flow

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-08-16
- Amended: 2026-11-26

## Decision

`microsimulator.flow` solves one pressure unknown per fluid x/y column. For gap height H and depth-averaged mobility m, the equations are `div_xy(q) = 0`, `q = -H m grad_xy(p)`. The default mobility is proportional to H², giving the required cubic gap conductance H³. Supplied mobility must be constant through each depth column. Harmonic face conductances represent series resistance. This is a lubrication/Darcy closure: it omits in-plane viscous stresses and requires a shallow gap and slowly varying geometry for physical accuracy. It is not the full Brinkman equation.

Supported masks have contiguous fluid columns above a common planar floor, with flow along x or y. Overhangs, vertically disconnected channels, and depth-varying mobility are rejected; they require resolved flow. H is the authored staircase height, not an exact continuous-device height. Geometry convergence remains necessary, particularly near sharp depth changes.

Pressure is held half a spacing outside each open exterior face, with no flux through any other. A single-axis solve opens the whole lower face of its axis at pressure one and the whole upper face at zero. A port solve opens the faces of its ports: each port is a rectangle of boundary sites on an x or y face that spans the full depth of the fluid behind it, and either carries a stated mean speed over its open area, positive into the grid, or holds a stated pressure. At least one port holds a pressure. Port fluxes are linear in port pressures, so the solver makes one pressure solve for each speed port held alone at pressure one, finds by a small dense solve the pressures that deliver the stated speeds, and returns that combination of the solved fields with each port's pressure, flux and mean speed. The combination conserves volume as each solve does. Port pressures are in the solver's units, relative to the mobility's scale. A port's face must be a fixed boundary; the face is closed to flow outside its ports, though transport still treats a fixed face as a reservoir wherever fluid touches it, so a device keeps the rest of a port's face solid. Native CPU, Metal, and CUDA operators solve the reduced system by Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate gradient. Accelerator vectors stay on device. Fields use binary32, with default pressure residual tolerance `1e-6`. Disconnected inlet/outlet geometry and periodic boundaries are rejected.

The depth-integrated horizontal face flux is distributed over the overlapping open depth. A reconstructed vertical flux balances horizontal redistribution within each column, while distributing the small pressure residual over depth and keeping floor and roof closed. This is a conservative transport reconstruction, not a resolved vertical velocity profile. Conservation holds to pressure-solve and floating-point error; pressure residual alone is not a relative flux error bound. For pressure-driven flow, the complete field is scaled to the requested mean inlet speed.

`colony_volume_fraction` deposits the conserved biochemical biomass B defined in ADR 0024, using exact voxel integrals of a separable tent kernel with a fixed physical averaging radius (default 4 length units). Masked support is restricted to a face-connected fluid component and renormalized to conserve amount. The deposit and its closures run natively on the selected backend in binary32 ([ADR 0030](0030-provider-interfaces.md)). The raw density is never clipped. `colony_mobility` depth-averages this density and caps it only inside the empirical Kozeny-Carman resistance closure. Its coefficient and averaging radius require calibration. `colony_species_density` deposits concentration times B with the same kernel.

The single-axis solver also accepts an in-plane body force per column. For the trap, `colony_flow_coupling` deposits biomass and biomass times center velocity with the same kernel, giving a depth-averaged biomass velocity V. With empty-device mobility b and empirical drag d, the momentum balance is `u/b = -grad(p) + d (V - u)`. Thus `m = b/(1+b*d)` and the integrated flux is `q = H m (-grad(p) + d V)`. Stationary cells recover the stationary mobility model. Rods translating with a uniform fluid produce zero relative drag. This closure averages translation over the kernel support and depth. Rotation, individual rod surfaces, and flow through the gaps between rods are unresolved.

At an interior face the solver multiplies harmonic conductance by the arithmetic mean of the two adjacent body forces, integrating the driving force over the two half cells in series. At an open boundary it uses the adjacent column's force, and closed faces carry zero flux. The negative divergence of this imposed flux enters a pressure solve with zero boundary pressures. The resulting divergence-free field is added at its physical amplitude to a pressure-driven solution whose pressure drop is chosen to meet the prescribed inlet speed. The reported iteration count sums the pressure solves and the residual is their maximum.

The trap samples center velocities from the last accepted contact substep every five steps. The next step solves for the flow before advancing mechanics and transport. Checkpoints taken between sampling and solving retain the velocities by cell ID. The refresh interval, kernel radius and drag law set the temporal and spatial accuracy of this lagged coupling. Other models can still select stationary biomass for `colony_mobility`.

## Validation

Tests cover inlets at different speeds on one face, an inlet on a face perpendicular to the outlet's, the agreement of a full-face port pair with the single-axis solve, port solves on every backend, cubic flux partition between unequal depths, flux continuity and uniform-tracer preservation across a depth step, invalid column geometry, mass-conserving deposition and exact aggregation under refinement, native backend conformance, and the uniform duct/pillar benchmarks. `scripts/run_flow_benchmarks.py` compares the closure with resolved flow in its shared regime. Shallow flow does not supply no-slip wall shear or near-wall rod velocities.
